# Showcase voice and video quality audit

Read-only source audit, 2026-10-09. No render, playback, listening, build, or deployment was performed for this audit. Recommendations below are proposed changes, not delivered quality gains.

## Current production path

- The published showcase offers 16 editions: eight MLAI montage cuts assembled from existing React film MP4s and eight dedicated Quesar architecture/studio films, each at 60, 120, 180, or 600 seconds. The montage edit preserves source audio by trimming and concatenating video/audio together; it does not produce a new voice performance. [../trailer-editions-2026-10-09/README.md:3-15; ../trailer-editions-2026-10-09/render.py:16-27,61-80; ../trailer-editions-2026-10-09/receipts/public-browser-acceptance.md:9-24]
- The interactive React cinematic rooms use on-demand browser Kokoro-82M via `kokoro-js`; persona voice IDs are Abbey `af_heart`, Aviva `bf_emma`, and Abi `am_michael`, with separate speed, gap, and EQ settings. The six encoded MLAI source films **also use Kokoro PCM**, generated in a local browser from the same model and persona voice IDs, then placed at cue starts and muxed with captured frames. Their `timing.json` files record measured duration and voice per cue. They are not macOS `say` films. This existing export is the closest reusable path for Quesar. [../../../src/cinematic/film/kokoro-loader.ts:1-13,47-55; ../../../src/cinematic/film/tokens.ts:41-69; ../../../src/cinematic/film/neural-voice.ts:59-100; ../../../scripts/narrated-film.ts:14-21,78-161,167-210; ../../../public/media/films/manifest.json:1-16; ../../../public/media/films/film.timing.json:1-21]
- The encoded Quesar films use macOS `say -v Samantha -r 155` once per chapter. Each chapter's full heading and body becomes one caption/narration cue, synthesized into AIFF, padded to its equal-length chapter slot, concatenated as mono 48 kHz WAV, then encoded to 192 kbps AAC. A cue that runs beyond its slot fails rendering. This arrangement can create different lengths of silence after chapters; that is an inference from the padding algorithm, not a listening finding. [README.md:3-5; render-native30.mjs:8-11]
- Quesar final masters capture a fresh 1920×1080 React frame at each 1/30-second time point and encode H.264 at 30 fps. The earlier 10 fps outputs are previews that repeat frames. [README.md:5,22-30; render-native30.mjs:2,11-13; render.mjs:2,11-12]
- In each dedicated cut, chapter selection divides the entire runtime into equal slots (15 seconds per chapter for the short cuts; 20 seconds for ten minutes). The React `Film` renders the same header, article, orb, four-node diagram, footer, and progress bar for every chapter. Its title enters over roughly the first eighth of a chapter; continuous motion is orb rotation, node opacity/translation, and the progress bar. The two editions change palette, grid proportions, and node labels, but share that composition and motion grammar. [render-native30.mjs:2,8; main.jsx:5-6; style.css:1]
- Chapter copy has source/status tags and explicitly labels illustrative diagrams. Proposed Studio, multimodal, and Network topics carry `VISION / ROADMAP` in the source; preserve those boundaries in any redesign. [create.py:5-34; README.md:7]

## Evidence limit

The saved browser receipt reports sampled muted playback, decoded frames, metadata, and captions for all 16 public films. Native Quesar receipts report full decode and exact 30 fps sampling. A prior reviewer inspected three stills in each of six shorter Quesar films and found them legible at 1080p, while noting small, lower-contrast secondary labels. These establish technical delivery and sampled still readability, not complete motion or vocal quality. Full-duration subjective listening remains unverified; the native verification receipt still marks `visualReview` and `listeningReview` pending. [../trailer-editions-2026-10-09/receipts/public-browser-acceptance.md:5-24,38; ../trailer-editions-2026-10-09/receipts/native-acceptance-report.md:5-16; ../trailer-editions-2026-10-09/receipts/delivery-audit.md:35-46; artifacts-native30/quesar-architecture-60/verification.json:151-155]

## Highest-value upgrade path

1. **Reuse the existing browser neural export.** Adapt `scripts/narrated-film.ts`'s loopback Playwright Kokoro synthesis, `PERSONAS` voice/speed selection, `pronounce` text conversion, measured PCM cue placement, hashes, overrun refusal, and FFmpeg muxing for the Quesar script. Replace `render-native30.mjs`'s chapter-by-chapter macOS `say` calls. Share the loader/model choice with `kokoro-loader.ts` rather than maintaining a second drifting CDN/model constant. Keep voice selection and model identity in each receipt; the prior MLAI manifest explicitly lacks an immutable model-weight revision. No new provider or production dependency is needed. [../../../scripts/narrated-film.ts:9-21,78-161,167-210; ../../../src/cinematic/film/kokoro-loader.ts:13-14,47-67; ../../../public/media/films/manifest.json:7-16; render-native30.mjs:8-11]
   The live `AudioEngine` also applies Abbey's low shelf, presence, and high shelf EQ through a bus compressor, plus gap insertion and sentence-edge fades. The current export script places raw Kokoro PCM with edge fades and fixed headroom; it does not apply that live EQ/compressor chain. Reuse the same settings in an offline audio processing step, or explicitly approve a different master sound after listening; do not claim parity from matching voice IDs. The live engine owns only an in-memory LRU buffer cache, while export receipts record cue hashes and measured PCM. [../../../src/cinematic/film/neural-voice.ts:59-74; ../../../src/lib/trailer-engine/audio.ts:380-405,431-481,533-583; ../../../scripts/narrated-film.ts:26-47,115-158]
2. **Time speech and captions to actual neural PCM.** Start with `quesar-architecture-60`: split each chapter into spoken phrases, measure generated waveforms, place intentional pauses at semantic boundaries, and derive caption windows from the approved cue timeline. The existing live narration controller already keys speech to cue starts and handles seeks; the production MP4 needs an explicit audio export because capture mode suppresses live speech. Listen to the full candidate for clarity, pronunciation, pace, and transitions before accepting the voice direction. [render-native30.mjs:8-11; ../../../src/cinematic/film/narration.tsx:52-93; ../../../src/cinematic/film/speech.ts:49-68; ../../../src/cinematic/film/capture.ts:16-22]
3. **Make chapters visually distinct.** In the same 60-second pilot, replace the universal four-row diagram in selected chapters with source-specific, clearly illustrative diagrams or a browser walkthrough where the site actually exposes that behavior. Build a small set of layouts (claim/evidence, system boundary, workflow, closing action), retaining source/status tags. This addresses the repeated composition directly without claiming live service traces. [main.jsx:5; style.css:1; create.py:15-24,28-34]
4. **Give each shot an event and a transition.** Drive scene entry, diagram reveal, camera/depth movement, and exit from a deterministic per-chapter timeline. Make motion correspond to the spoken idea, then check actual frame sequences around chapter boundaries rather than only start/middle/end JPEGs. Preserve native 30 fps sampling and the existing frame/decode receipt. The site's established capture mode already supplies a deterministic 30 fps clock and frame readiness handling that could be reused when these scenes move into the live React showcase. [main.jsx:5-6; render-native30.mjs:12-13; ../../../src/cinematic/film/capture.ts:3-27,144-218; ../trailer-editions-2026-10-09/receipts/delivery-audit.md:35-46]
5. **Accept quality explicitly before scaling.** Review the complete 60-second pilot with headphones and at mobile playback size; record time-coded audio/visual defects and sign off the exact audio/video hashes. Only then apply the chosen voice direction and shot grammar across the remaining durations and editions, regenerate captions/transcripts and public assets, and rerun technical plus browser checks. The existing receipts intentionally do not equate full decode with listening or visual acceptance.
